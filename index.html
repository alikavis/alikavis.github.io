About

I am a Senior Researcher at the Optimization & Decision Intelligence (ODI) Group at ETH Zürich. I received my PhD from EPFL in August 2023, where I was advised by Volkan Cevher. Prior to joining ETH, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow at UT Austin, affiliated with the IFML and the TRIPODS Institute.

My research centers on the design and analysis of universal and parameter-free algorithms for stochastic optimization. I aim to build methods that work well across diverse problem settings without requiring practitioners to hand-tune hyperparameters such as learning rates or noise estimates. My work spans first-order and second-order methods, extensions to games and minimax problems, and applications in deep learning.

Research

A central goal of my work is closing the gap between the idealized conditions assumed in optimization theory and the messy, parameter-ignorant reality of applied machine learning.

Universal & Parameter-Free Optimization

Algorithms that achieve optimal convergence rates across convex and non-convex settings without knowledge of problem-specific constants such as smoothness or noise variance.

Adaptive First-Order Methods

Fully adaptive SGD variants (STORM+, AdaGrad-type methods, variance reduction) that self-tune to problem geometry and noise without manual learning-rate selection.

Second-Order & Accelerated Methods

Noise-adaptive second-order algorithms (Extra-Newton and beyond) that exploit curvature information while remaining practical under stochastic noise and inexact oracle access.

Minimax & Game Optimization

Optimal and adaptive methods for two-player games and minimax problems, including second-order optimistic approaches and the theoretical role of alternation in weakening adversarial dynamics.
